01· The contest

Five days.
One seat each.

Teams of any size take one open role and implement it against its published contract. There is no track, no theme, and no pitch. A seat is filled when something runs and someone else can point their client at it.

01 · Build

Read the contract

Every seat is specified in public, technology-neutral prose. You are free to ignore the reference implementation entirely.

The contract is the brief.

02 · Submit

Hand over an endpoint

A repository, a running instance, and the credentials to exercise it. Slides are not a submission.

Code nobody can run is a proposal.

03 · Judge

One arbiter, in writing

Conformance first, then durability. Every verdict is published with its reasons, including the refusals.

The arbiter owes reasons, not applause.

04 · Fill

Homestead the seat

You own what you built and whatever it earns. No assignment of rights, no equity, no claim by the organizer.

The week ends; the seat keeps working.

How to read this memo

One line of text on a public ledger. The first field names the event, the second is p for in person or r for remote, and the third is a sixteen-bit hex mask — one bit per seat, in the order below, lowest bit first. Anyone can decode it without asking us.
